How I Think

Start from the problem

Before architecture, I want three things: who it's for, what constrains it, and the metric that will say it worked. Discovery and prioritization are engineering work — not paperwork that happens before it.

AI has to earn trust

An AI feature isn't done when it demos well. It's done when its output is validated, its failures are measured, and a person can tell what it did and why. Most of my recent work lives in that gap between a good demo and a system you'd actually ship.

Depth underneath

The AI work sits on a distributed-systems and SQL foundation, and that's deliberate. When something breaks at scale, the foundation is what finds it — the model rarely is.

Write it down, teach it

I write up what I learn (one piece below) and run sessions helping other engineers adopt AI-assisted development. Explaining a thing is how I find out whether I actually understand it.
